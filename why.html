why five days

dating means going on dates.

We think people should meet, not match.

The best introductions we've seen didn't come from an app. They came from a friend who knew two people well enough to say: you two, Thursday, this place.

[Kai & Ruby: one or two true sentences about the introduction, or the evening, that made you start this.]

So we do it by hand. We read every answer. We pick the person, the place and the time. You get a text, and a table.

It doesn't scale, and we've stopped trying to make it. We only open a city where we can be in the room. New York first, then every major walkable city, because the best dates end with a walk somewhere.

If the dates land, there's something here. If they don't, the hunch was wrong, and we'd rather know in six weeks than in two years.

&

what we crossed out

The usual parts of a dating product, and what we do instead.

  • A number can't tell you whether someone wants the same Wednesday you do.

  • Two people read every answer.

  • One person, one evening, planned.

  • We look to check you're real. That's all we do with it.

  • We don't. The privacy notice says so in writing.
